# Game of Food

**Game of Food** is a fun and simple game where players take on the roles of different species—animals or plants, carnivorous or herbivorous—and try to "eat" the species of the other player. It’s a creative and strategic game that encourages players to think about ecosystems, food chains, and survival tactics.

## Origin of the Game

This game was born during a brainstorming session with my 8-year-old daughter on a long bus ride. It started as a playful way to pass the time and quickly evolved into a collaborative project. The video version of the game was created in partnership with her, making it a special family project.

## How to Play

1. **Choose your first Species**: Each player selects a species. It can be an animal or a plant, carnivorous or herbivorous.
    - Carnivorous species start with 2 life points.
    - Herbivorous species start with 3 life points.
2. **Turn**: Each player takes turns making moves. A player can do each one of the following action:
    - "feed" (for a plant or an herbivore)
    - "add" (a species) or "attack" (for an animal or a carnivore plant)
3. **Feeding**: By feeding a species, the player can add one life point to either a plant or an herbivore. This action is crucial for survival and growth.
4. **Adding a species**: If selected, the player can add a new species up to a maximum of 5 species. This allows for strategic planning and diversification of the ecosystem.
5. **Attacking**: If a player chooses to eat, they can attempt to eat another player’s species. The success of this action depends on the type of species and their respective life points.
   - If the attacking species is herbivorous: the target has to be a plant species loses 1 life point and the attacking species gains 1 life point.
   - If the attacking species is carnivorous and the target is herbivorous: players play rock-paper-scissors. If the attacking species wins, the target loses 1 life point and the attacking species gains 1 life point. If the attacking species loses, the attacking species loses 1 life point and the target does not gain or loose life.
   - If the attacking species is carnivorous and the target is carnivorous: players play rock-paper-scissors. If the attacking species wins, the target loses 1 life point and the attacking species gains 2 life points. If the attacking species loses, the attacking species loses 1 life point and the target gains 1 life point.
6. **End of Game**: The game never ends in a win or loss. Instead, it’s about the fun of playing and the interactions between species.

## Features of the Video Version

The computer version of **Game of Food** brings the game to life with interactive elements. It’s designed to be engaging for players of all ages, combining fun with a touch of education about nature and ecosystems.
